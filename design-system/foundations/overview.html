Design system

Moodcraft

A private, single-person tool for recording mood and medication, so the relationship between the two becomes visible.

Descriptive, not evaluative

The app states what the data shows. It never recommends a dose, a gap, or a course of action — so the interface never uses approval/warning language, checkmarks-as-verdicts, or red alert colors. The person interprets; the screen just makes the pattern visible.

Calm at 2am

Recording is irregular, driven by medication use rather than a daily ritual, and often happens late. Dark-only, low-contrast-but-legible, no urgency cues, nothing that nags or notifies.

Absence is information

A day with no record is unknown, never zero. A day with no intake means the medication was not taken, trusted as fact. The interface always has a distinct visual state for “nothing was recorded here” that is not the same as “recorded as fine.”

System at a glance

Dark-only · mobile-first, desktop-adapted · one accent hue for interaction, a separate hue for severity, a separate pair for event valence, a separate hue for dose markers — four color families that never overlap in meaning. No drop shadows. Generous rounding. A single humanist sans throughout, scaled up and loosened for the sentences the app writes about your data.
